GroundLevel SDK

A JavaScript library with the engines behind GroundLevel: 31 UK cases you can search, a 7-strand research weave that also flags where the authorities point different ways, a 13-rule compliance check (UK GDPR, the EU AI Act, employment, consumer and health and safety law) and seven court-document drafts.

It is the GroundLevel page's own code, taken out so a developer, or an AI agent, can call it directly. It gives information, not legal advice: the case notes are short summaries, and the match score measures keyword relevance, not your chance of winning.

Start here

  1. Install it from GitHub (it is not on the npm registry): 
    npm install github:sjgant80-hub/groundlevel-sdk
  2. Import what you need and call it:
    import { searchCases, runWeave, scoreRisk, auditCompliance } from '@ai-native-solutions/groundlevel-sdk';

const hits = searchCases('landlord did not protect my deposit', 'housing');
const weave = await runWeave('employer dismissed me without process', 'unfair_dismissal');
const match = scoreRisk(weave);            // { label: 'STRONG' | 'MODERATE' | 'WEAK' | 'MINIMAL', pct, recommendation, basis }
const audit = auditCompliance({ ca_uses_ai: 'yes', ca_eu_users: 'yes', ca_audit_log: 'no' });
  3. Or use it in a browser page with no install: 
    import * as sdk from 'https://sjgant80-hub.github.io/groundlevel-sdk/src/index.js';
  4. Pin a commit for production (npm install github:sjgant80-hub/groundlevel-sdk#<commit>) so what you tested is what runs.

How it works

  1. The cases, the weave patterns, the compliance rules and the drafting templates are the GroundLevel page's own. scripts/parity.mjs reads the published page and fails the build if a single case, rule, pattern or wording differs; the 13 rules are also compared verdict by verdict on 4,000 sets of answers.
  2. The search scores a case by where your words appear: anywhere in its text (2), in its tags (3) or in its principle (3). Words under three letters do not count.
  3. The weave asks the same question from seven angles (strands): constitutional, liberty, criminal and regulatory, equity, housing, employment and commerce, cross-border. A pattern weights the strands. Where two strands' top cases are in the same area with different outcomes, it says so: that is a conflict you need to deal with.
  4. The match score averages the strands' top relevance, takes 8 off for each conflict, and labels the result. It measures how much of the bundled case set fits your words. It is not a prediction of the outcome.
  5. Every function is tested, and a mutation gate flips each comparison and condition in the code to check a test notices. The build fails unless every flip is caught, or the gate records in writing why that flip cannot change any result.

What it costs, against what you pay now

There is no licence fee (MIT) and nothing metered. Against a per-seat legal research product, this does not replace the full reports and commentary; it replaces the first sort: which cases are likely relevant, where the authorities conflict, which compliance gaps your answers show, and a first draft to edit. The cost is your developers' time to wire it in.

Security and data protection

No network calls, no storage, no telemetry: the functions take your text and return results in memory. The only exception is optional and yours: if you pass your own aiComplete function, the weave or the draft is sent to whichever model you call there. Personal data you pass stays in your process.

Compliance

It does not give legal advice and does not make anyone a regulated adviser. The compliance check flags likely gaps against a fixed rule list; it is a checklist, not an audit. The Article 12 rule reflects the dates of Regulation (EU) 2026/1744: high-risk logging applies from 2 December 2027 (Annex III) or 2 August 2028 (Annex I products).

Risks, answered straight

  • The 31 cases are short summaries, not the judgments. Two of them (Spencer v Taylor and Caridon) are about section 21, which was abolished for private tenancies in England on 1 May 2026; both are marked historical.
  • The match percentage measures keyword relevance against 31 cases. It is not a probability of success and the page says so.
  • The compliance rules are a fixed checklist. A clean score means none of these 13 gaps applies to your answers, not that you are compliant.
  • The drafts are templates to edit, not finished court documents.
  • The match score has not been measured against real case outcomes. It ranks the bundled cases by keyword relevance; nobody has checked how often a STRONG match wins.
  • Content is as at 17 November 2026 (the section 21 cases and the Article 12 date were reviewed that day). Law changes land on the GroundLevel page first; this SDK's weekly parity check then fails until it carries them, so it cannot quietly fall behind the page.
  • Scope: the cases are UK (mostly England and Wales) with a few EU ones; the compliance rules cover UK GDPR, the EU AI Act and UK employment, consumer and safety law. Text in English.
  • No third-party certification or audit. The evidence is the public CI record: every push runs the tests, the mutation gate and the parity check where anyone can read the result.

The explainer

Most "SDKs" that sit next to a product page are a copy that drifts: the page gets fixed, the copy does not. This one cannot drift unnoticed. The parity check reads the published page and compares it, piece by piece, with the modules here; CI runs it on every push and every week.

The second problem with copied code is that nobody knows whether its tests test anything. The mutation gate answers that: it breaks the code on purpose, one comparison at a time, and fails the build if the tests still pass. When this SDK was repaired in November 2026, the inherited tests caught 25 of 104 such breaks. The current code has 103; the new tests catch 102, and the gate has a written reason for the last: a loop bound that cannot change any result.

The repair also corrected the product: the score used to print "estimated success rates" (70-95%, 40-65%, 15-35%) that nothing had measured, and the Article 12 rule gave an AI Act date that the Digital Omnibus moved. Both the page and the SDK now say what is actually measured, and when the rule actually applies.
